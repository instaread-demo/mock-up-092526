If you’ve typed “canva quiz maker” or “google forms quiz maker” into Google recently, you probably landed on the tool’s own marketing page — not an honest comparison. That’s not an accident: both companies rank for their own product name. So here’s the comparison neither of them will give you.

The short version: Canva and Google Forms are both quiz builders, not quiz hosts. They’re excellent at the thing they’re designed for — a polished single-question graphic, or a self-graded assessment someone fills out on their own time. Neither one was built to put a question on a screen in front of a room and watch a leaderboard update in real time. That’s a different job, and it’s the one TriviaMaker’s online quiz maker is built around.

Quick answer

TriviaMaker is the best overall pick — it’s the only one of the three that covers every use case: live, hosted play with a real-time leaderboard and self-paced, assignable quizzes for async work. Canva is limited to one polished quiz graphic with no hosting. Google Forms only does self-paced grading, with no live element at all.

When Google Forms is genuinely the right call

Google Forms has a built-in quiz mode: flip a toggle in Settings, mark correct answers, and it auto-grades every submission with no manual work. That makes it the right tool when the goal is assessment, not engagement — a homework check, a certification test, an onboarding knowledge check someone completes on their own schedule.

It’s free, it’s already inside Google Workspace, and most people already know how to use it. The trade-off is that it was never designed to be watched. There’s no live leaderboard, no host-paced “reveal the answer together” moment, and no way to show a room of people how everyone’s doing in real time — Google’s own support forums confirm this is a workaround-only feature, not something built in. If you’re grading async work, that’s irrelevant. If you’re trying to energize a room, it’s the whole problem.

When Canva is genuinely the right call

Canva’s quiz maker is a design tool first. You can build a single-question, text-based quiz — multiple choice, true/false, fill-in-the-blank — with up to 10 answer options, dropped straight into a presentation, social post, or lesson slide using Canva’s template and image library. If what you need is one sharp-looking quiz graphic for Instagram or a lesson opener, nothing else on this list will make it look better, faster.

What it isn’t built for: live hosting. There’s no scoreboard, no buzzer, no way for a group to compete against each other in real time — results export as a CSV or a static image, not a live view. Free accounts get up to 50 AI-generated quizzes a month; beyond that it’s a Pro feature. It’s a design tool wearing a quiz costume, and for a single polished piece of content, that’s exactly what you want.

Where both tools run out of road

Here’s the pattern: neither Canva nor Google Forms was built to answer the question a lot of searchers actually have — “how do I run this live, in front of people, with some competitive energy?” A teacher running a Friday review session, a corporate trainer opening a workshop, or an event host running pub trivia all need the same three things:

  • A leaderboard the whole room can see update in real time
  • A way for participants to join instantly — no app download, no account creation
  • A format built for competition, not just a list of questions on a slide

Google Forms doesn’t do live leaderboards at all. Canva doesn’t do live hosting at all. That gap is exactly where a purpose-built live quiz platform earns its keep.

The honest caveat: if you only need to check whether people read the material, Google Forms is faster to set up than anything else here. If you only need one gorgeous quiz graphic for a single post, Canva wins on design polish every time. Neither claim below is “TriviaMaker is better at everything” — it’s better at the live, hosted, competitive use case specifically.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a quiz in Canva for free?

Yes. Canva’s free plan includes its quiz maker with single-question, text-based quizzes and up to 50 AI-generated quizzes a month. Pro accounts get a higher AI generation limit and more branding options.

Does Google Forms have a live quiz mode with a leaderboard?

No — Google Forms’ quiz mode is built for self-paced, auto-graded assessments. There’s no native live leaderboard or host-paced play; people looking for that typically pair Forms with a separate live-hosting tool or switch to a platform built for it.

What’s the best free alternative to Kahoot for teams?

TriviaMaker offers a free-forever plan with 7 quiz formats (versus Kahoot’s single trivia format on its free tier) and doesn’t require participants to create accounts. See our full Kahoot vs TriviaMaker comparison for a detailed breakdown.

Can I use Canva or Google Forms for corporate training quizzes?

Yes, for knowledge checks people complete on their own time. For interactive, group-based training sessions where you want engagement and a live leaderboard, a hosted format works better — see our guide to quiz tools for corporate training.

Do participants need to download an app to join a TriviaMaker live quiz?

No. Participants join by scanning a QR code or entering a short join code in a browser on any phone, tablet, or laptop — no app, no account required.
