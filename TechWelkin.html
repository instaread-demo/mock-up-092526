Fix iPhone Microphone Problems

You answer an important phone call, say hello, and are immediately met with the frustrating response: “Hello? Are you there? I can’t hear you.” You shout into the phone, but the person on the other end only hears static, muffling, or complete silence.

When your iPhone microphone fails, it renders the device entirely useless for its original, primary function: communication. Siri stops responding to your voice, your Voice Notes record dead air, and your FaceTime calls become one-sided pantomimes.

Before you assume the microphone is permanently broken, you need to understand that your iPhone does not just have one microphone; it has several. Depending on exactly what app you are using, the phone seamlessly switches between them. Fixing the issue requires figuring out exactly which microphone is acting up and clearing away the physical or software issues preventing it from hearing you.

Step 1: The Three-Microphone Diagnostic Test

To fix the problem, you must first isolate it. Modern iPhones typically have three primary microphones: one at the bottom (for regular phone calls), one inside the top earpiece (for speakerphone and Siri), and one on the back near the camera array (for noise cancellation and video recording).

You can test them individually to pinpoint the failure:

  1. Test the Primary Bottom Microphone: Open the Voice Memos app. Hold the phone normally and speak directly into the bottom of the device. Play the memo back. If you hear static or nothing at all, your primary phone call mic is blocked or broken.
  2. Test the Front Top Microphone: Open the Camera app and switch to the front-facing “Selfie” video mode. Record a quick video of yourself talking. Play it back. If it is silent, the top microphone is failing.
  3. Test the Rear Microphone: Switch to the rear-facing video camera. Tap the back of the phone gently with your finger while speaking, then play the video back.

If all three microphones record complete silence, you almost certainly have a software crash. If only one microphone fails the test, you have a physical blockage or a localized hardware failure.

Step 2: Excavating the Microphone Grilles

If your bottom microphone failed the Voice Memo test, physical blockage is the most likely culprit. The primary microphone sits behind the tiny circular grilles at the bottom of your iPhone, right next to the charging port.

Just as pocket lint can ruin your charging capabilities — which we covered in our guide on what to do when your iPhone refuses to charge –dust, dirt, and makeup can easily clog these microscopic microphone holes.

  1. Find a clean, dry, soft-bristled toothbrush. Do not use a needle or a toothpick. Sticking a sharp object into the microphone hole will permanently puncture the delicate audio membrane.
  2. Turn the phone off.
  3. Gently scrub the bottom grilles to dislodge any compacted dust.
  4. Check the rear microphone. It is a tiny, pin-sized hole located right next to the camera lenses on the back glass. Gently brush it out as well.

Step 3: Remove the Case and Screen Protector

A poorly manufactured phone case is the silent killer of iPhone microphones.

If you recently bought a new case, especially a rugged, heavy-duty, or waterproof one, it might not be perfectly aligned with the microphone cutouts. Even a millimeter of misalignment can muffle your voice or block the rear noise-canceling microphone, causing your voice to sound distant or robotic to the person on the other end of the call.

Furthermore, if you applied a thick tempered glass screen protector and accidentally laid it over the front top notch (covering the earpiece slit), you have physically suffocated the front microphone.

Remove the phone from its case entirely and peel off the screen protector if it covers the top notch. Make a test call. If your voice is suddenly crystal clear, your accessories are the problem, not your phone.

Step 4: The Invisible Bluetooth Hijack

If your Voice Memos and video recordings work perfectly, but you are completely silent on actual phone calls, your audio input is likely being hijacked by a Bluetooth device.

As we discussed in our guide on diagnosing missing sound on an iPhone, iOS aggressively clings to Bluetooth connections. If your wireless earbuds are sitting on a desk in the next room, or your phone is still connected to your car’s stereo system parked in the driveway, the iPhone is using their microphones, not its own.

Swipe down from the top right of your screen to open the Control Center, tap the blue Bluetooth icon to turn it off temporarily, and make a test call. If the person can hear you now, you just need to manage your Bluetooth connections in your settings.

Step 5: Fixing Software Glitches and the Audio IC Failure

If your microphones are perfectly clean, your Bluetooth is off, but the phone still refuses to record your voice, the audio routing software has likely crashed.

You must execute a hardware-level force restart to reboot the audio drivers. Quickly press and release Volume Up, then Volume Down, and then press and hold the Side Power button until the screen goes black and the Apple logo appears.

The Audio IC Red Flag: If you restart the phone and the microphones are still completely dead, you must look for a specific warning sign. Make a standard phone call. Look at the screen. Is the “Speaker” icon illuminated, or is it completely greyed out and un-tappable?

If the Speaker icon is greyed out, and your Voice Memos app immediately crashes or refuses to press “record,” you have suffered an Audio IC (Integrated Circuit) failure. This means the microchip on your logic board responsible for processing sound has physically failed or detached from the board. This is a severe hardware failure, commonly resulting from drops or internal bending. It cannot be fixed with software; you must take the device to an Apple Store or an authorized microsoldering expert for motherboard repair.
