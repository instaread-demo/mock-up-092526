The best headphone brands aren’t just about logos or hype, they’re the ones that let you hear music the way it was meant to be heard.

I still remember the moment I knew I had the right headphones: I started catching tiny details in my mix that had been hiding all along, and suddenly I was making the right adjustments instead of second-guessing. That’s the kind of difference the headphone you choose can make, and it’s why picking from the top headphone brands really matters.

In this guide, I’ll share rankings built from over 57,800 ratings and reviews by musicians, engineers, and audio enthusiasts, so instead of just my ears, you’re getting the collective experience of people who live and breathe sound.

While this list focuses on the best headset brands used in music and audio production (rather than every style of consumer headphones out there), I also took into account essentials like noise isolation, battery life, and audio quality. If you’re curious about which great headphone brands deliver that kind of clarity and reliability, you’re in the right place.

6 Best Headphone Brands for Musicians

AKG – Best Headphone Brands Number 6

This is short for Akustische und Kino-Geräte Gesellschaft, which was founded in 1949 in Austria by two entrepreneurs. One a physicist and an engineer. AKG started as a company that produced audio equipment for movie theaters but quickly expanded into the professional audio world. By the early 1950s, AKG was a pioneer in the industry, inventing the dynamic cardioid microphone in 1953, a piece of technology still widely used today.

AKG began producing over ear headphones in 1949, making them one of the first companies to focus on best branded headphones for audio production. Their early focus on producing equipment for professionals set the stage for their reputation as a great headphone brand. Over the years, AKG continues to be a strong contender for the best headphones brand title. It also maintained its strong presence in the music industry, known for their durable, high-quality microphones, and different types of headphones.

The AKG K240, a semi-open model, is an excellent choice for anyone new to music production. These headphones have a reputation for providing balanced and accurate sound, making them ideal for critical listening tasks such as mixing and mastering. The semi-open design allows for a more natural soundstage, which can help with stereo imaging—useful when you’re fine-tuning tracks. These headphones are a solid choice for those looking for good sound quality.

Notable Musicians and Producers: AKG’s reputation extends beyond just home studios. Musicians and producers like Bootsy Collins, Steve Pageot, Sweet Sweet Moon, and Ghost Capsules rely on AKG headphones sound and microphones for both live performances and studio recording.

For someone starting out in music production, AKG offers a best brand in headphones that combines professional-grade quality and affordability. Additionally, their closed back headphones ensure passive noise isolation in recording sessions.

KRK Systems – Best Headphone Brands Number 5

KRK Systems may be more famous for their studio monitors, but their expertise in speaker technology has made their headphones a top headphone brand for music producers and engineers who need balanced sound and audio quality. Founded in 1986 by Keith R. Klawitter, KRK Systems started with a focus on building high-quality studio monitors. Klawitter, an audio engineer for Hollywood films, wanted monitors that provided better sound quality and clarity, so he built his own—and KRK was born.

KRK Systems took their expertise in studio monitors and applied it to over ear headphones, creating products that are perfect for critical listening in quiet environments. The KRK KNS 8402 headphones are one of their best headphone models, designed to deliver the same accurate sound you’d expect from their studio monitors. They provide excellent noise isolation, meaning you can focus on the details of your mix without being distracted by background noise. These wired headphones excel in capturing detailed sound for professionals.

Sennheiser – Best Headphone Brands Number 4

Sennheiser was founded just after World War II, in 1945, by Fritz Sennheiser in a small laboratory in Germany. Originally named Laboratorium Wennebostel, the company initially focused on producing microphones. It wasn’t long before they became a major player in the audio equipment world. One of their most significant breakthroughs came in 1968 when they introduced the world’s first open back audiophile headphones. This set a new standard for how headphones could be used in both professional and consumer settings.

Sennheiser has always been at the forefront of audio innovation. This is why they remain a family-owned company with a stellar reputation. The Sennheiser HD 600 model, introduced in the 1990s, is still considered one of the best branded headphones for studio. These headphones provide incredibly transparent and fantastic sound quality, making them perfect for critical listening tasks like mixing and mastering. The over ear design allows air and sound to flow more freely. As a result it helps create a more natural and wide soundstage, giving you a clearer sense of the spatial arrangement in your recordings.

Audio-Technica – Best Headphone Brands Number 3

Audio-Technica has been a top headphone brand in the audio world since 1962. This is when they began as a manufacturer of phonograph cartridges in Tokyo.

Over the years, they branched out into making a wide range of audio equipment. The gear including microphones, turntables, and of course, headphones.

It wasn’t until 1974, though, that Audio-Technica introduced their first headphones. Since then, they have become one of the best headphone brands in professional audio.

The Audio-Technica ATH-R70x is one of their best headphones brand models for professional use. They are known for their comfort, clarity, and durability. These headphones are ideal for extended studio sessions due to their lightweight design. Their over ear nature ensures a broad, natural soundstage. This makes the ATH-R70x an excellent choice for mixing. Especially, where you want to be able to hear each element of your track clearly, offering good sound quality.

Sony – Best Headphone Brands Number 2

Sony is a massive company with a long and storied history, and their influence in the audio world is undeniable. Founded in 1946 in Tokyo, Sony has been a trailblazer in consumer electronics. They’ve made substantial contributions to professional audio as well. One of their early innovations was the development of the first in-ear headphones. This started in 1982. Since then they have continued to push the boundaries of audio technology ever since.

The Sony MDR-7506 is one of the most iconic wired headphones in the industry. It has been a staple in recording studios since its release. It’s known for its rugged build and precise sound profile reproduction. These closed-back headphones provide excellent noise isolation. This makes them perfect for monitoring during recording sessions or for listening in noisy environments. They deliver a clean, detailed sound, which makes them a favorite for tracking, mixing, and general studio use.

Beyerdynamic – Best Headphone Brands Number 1

Founded in 1924 in Berlin, Beyerdynamic is one of the oldest best headphone brands in the industry. They are known for their high-quality audio equipment. They started by producing speakers for movie theaters and later expanded into the realm of dynamic microphones and headphones. Their first pair of dynamic headphones, the DT48, was released in 1937 and remained in production until 2013. This is a testament to Beyerdynamic’s dedication to sound quality and longevity.

The Beyerdynamic DT 770 PRO is a closed-back model that is favored by musicians and producers. They have excellent noise isolation, clear, punchy bass, and balanced sound. These over ear headphones are ideal for tracking. This is important for when you need to focus on the sound coming through the headphones without any interference from ambient noise. The DT 770 PRO is known for its comfort, making it a great choice for recording sessions or long listening sessions. The high level of detailed sound in the mix allows you to catch every nuance. They are a popular choice in studios around the world and solidifying Beyerdynamic’s position as a top headphone brand.

Understanding How the Best Headphone Brands Were Ranked

The ranking process for these top headphone brands was comprehensive, relying on an analysis of more than 57,800 ratings and reviews from musicians, audio engineers, and home recording enthusiasts. This diverse range of opinions helped provide an accurate overview of which best headphone brands excel in the music production world. We also considered factors such as noise isolation, audio quality, battery life, and spatial audio when evaluating each brand’s performance.

What We Considered:

  • Eligibility Criteria: To be considered, each brand had to have headphones available for sale through a major U.S. retailer specializing in Pro Audio or Music Equipment. The headphones must also have been included in one of our guides focused on musician-specific needs, such as studio headphones, budget studio headphones, or headphones for recording and mixing.
  • Product Scope: Sixty-five different sets of headphones from the listed best brands for headphones were evaluated. These brands included AKG, Audio-Technica, Beyerdynamic, KRK Systems, Sennheiser, and Sony.
  • Rating and Review Sources: Data was compiled from various sources, including star ratings, written reviews, online forums, video reviews, and magazine articles. The reviews came from musicians, home recording enthusiasts, and professional audio engineers.
  • Process: Our team of experts analyzed the data using the Gearank algorithm, which assigns a score out of 100 based on individual product ratings. This score was then adjusted to account for the number of times each brand was recommended in our guides. Popular headphone brands were ranked according to their overall weighted average score.

Why the Best Headphone Brands Matter

Whether you’re a professional musician, an audio engineer, or a beginner just starting in music production, choosing the best budget studio headphones can make a huge difference in the quality of your work. And a big part of this choice is knowing the best headphone brand options.

All of the brands listed here have earned their place through years of innovation, high-quality products, and a commitment to meeting the needs of musicians and producers. Best headphones for professionals usually come with features like active noise cancellation, noise isolation, and accurate sound profiles, and these premium headphones are made to last through heavy use.

Best Headphone Brand Choices for Every Level

If you’re just starting out, brands like Audio-Technica and AKG offer reliable and affordable options that provide great value without sacrificing sound quality. For those ready to invest in more advanced gear, Sennheiser, Sony, and Beyerdynamic provide best branded headphones trusted by some of the biggest names in the industry. They offer the best studio headphones, ideal for mixing, mastering, and general music production.

No matter your budget or experience level, any of the headphones from these best brands for headphones will help elevate your music production, giving you the tools you need to create and enjoy high-quality sound. These are great headphone brands that consistently deliver performance and reliability, from wireless headphones with impressive battery life to noise canceling headphones that block out ambient sound.  

Wireless Headphones, Earphones, and Professional Applications

Many of these brands also offer earphones and wireless headphone model options. When comparing headphones vs earphones, headphones are still favored for professional use.

Popular wireless earphones from Apple, and wireless headphones like the Shure Aonic 70 and Bose 700 ANC, can be used – but they aren’t optimized for tracking, mixing, and mastering. You’ll notice substantial improvements in listening experience when switching from budget earbuds and earphones.
